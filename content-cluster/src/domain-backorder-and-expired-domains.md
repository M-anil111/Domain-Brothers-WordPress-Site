A domain backorder is a request to register a name the moment its current owner lets it go. It can win a taken name without negotiation, but only if the owner fails to renew. This guide covers the expiry lifecycle, backorder services, auctions, the risks of expired domains and simpler alternatives. It is part of our guide on [how to buy a domain name for a website](/how-to-buy-a-domain-name-for-a-website/).

!takeaways In short
- A name only becomes available after it passes through several stages after expiry, which typically take two to three months.
- A domain backorder service tries to register the name the instant it is deleted; success is never guaranteed.
- Popular expiring names usually go to auction, where the price can rise well above a backorder fee.
- Check the history of any expired name for trademarks, spam and penalties before you bid.

## How a domain expires: the lifecycle

Every registration has an expiry date. If the owner does not renew, the name does not become free the next day. It moves through a series of stages, and exact timings vary by registrar and by extension. For a typical generic top-level domain such as .com, the sequence looks like this.

| Stage | Typical length | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Expiry | Day 0 | The registration term ends. Many registrars stop the website and email from working. |
| Grace period | Varies by registrar, often around 30 to 45 days | The owner can still renew at the normal price. Some registrars auction the name during this time. |
| Redemption period | Typically about 30 days | The name is removed from the zone. The owner can restore it, usually for an extra fee. |
| Pending delete | Typically about 5 days | Nobody can renew or restore the name. It is waiting to be released. |
| Deleted (dropped) | One moment | The registry releases the name, and the first valid registration request wins. |

ICANN's [Expired Registration Recovery Policy](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/errp-2013-02-28-en) sets minimum rules registrars must follow for generic domains, including renewal reminders and a window in which the owner can still recover the name. Country-code domains follow their own registry rules, so check the specific extension before you plan around a date.

## What is a domain backorder?

A domain backorder is a standing instruction you give to a service: "If this name is deleted, try to register it for me." The service watches the name through its expiry stages. When the name drops, it sends registration requests to the registry as fast as it can.

Most domain backorder services charge only if they succeed, but terms differ, so read them before you place an order. Some registrars also offer their own backorder feature, which may only work for names held at that registrar. A backorder does not reserve the name, and it does not stop the current owner from renewing.

### How a domain backorder competes with others

Many people can backorder the same name, often through different services. Two things decide who gets it:

- **Speed at the drop.** Services that run many registrar connections send more requests at the moment of deletion. This practice is called drop catching.
- **Auction among backorders.** If a single service receives more than one domain backorder for the same name and catches it, it commonly holds a private auction among those customers.

The [Wikipedia article on domain drop catching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_drop_catching) gives more background on how the practice developed.

## Drop catching and expired domain auctions

Not every valuable name reaches the deleted stage. Some registrars place expiring names in auctions during the grace period, under terms in their registration agreement. If the original owner does not renew, the highest bidder gets the name without waiting for the drop.

This means there are three routes to buy expired domains:

1. **Expired domain auctions** run during the grace period, usually by or with the registrar.
2. **Drop catching**, where a domain backorder service registers the name the moment it is deleted.
3. **Hand registration** after the drop, which only works for names nobody else wanted.

For a name with obvious appeal, expect competition. A backorder fee is small, but an auction among several backorder holders can push the price much higher. Set a ceiling before bidding starts and stick to it.

Read the auction rules before you bid. Check whether the winning bid is binding, when payment is due, whether the original owner can still renew, and how the name reaches your account.

Some auctions settle the name at the auction house's own registrar first, so you may need to transfer it out later. A new registration or a transfer can also come with a 60-day lock on moving the name to another registrar.

Keep emotion out of the bidding. Auctions reward patience and a fixed budget. If the price passes your ceiling, let the name go and move on to your backup list.

## Risks of buying expired domains

An expired domain has a past. Sometimes that past is useful, such as existing links or type-in visitors. Sometimes it is a liability. Check these points before you place a domain backorder or a bid.

- **Trademark conflict.** A name that matches a registered brand can be challenged and taken from you. Our guide to [trademarks and domain disputes](/trademarks-and-domain-disputes-udrp-basics/) explains the UDRP process.
- **Spam or penalty history.** Look at old versions of the site in a web archive. Signs of spam, scraped content or link schemes are a warning.
- **Email still pointed at the name.** People may still send email to addresses on the old name. Do not use that mail for anything, and consider whether it creates a privacy problem for you.
- **Expectations about traffic and SEO.** Old links do not automatically pass value to a new site. Search engines may treat an expired name with a new owner as a fresh start. Our article on whether [premium domains help SEO](/do-premium-domains-help-seo/) explains what a name can and cannot do.
- **The owner renews.** The owner can renew during the grace period or restore during redemption. Your domain backorder then simply never fills.

## How to place a domain backorder, step by step

If the name is worth trying for, the process is short.

1. **Check the current status.** A WHOIS or RDAP lookup shows the expiry date and status codes such as redemption period or pending delete.
2. **Research the name's history.** Run the trademark, archive and spam checks above.
3. **Choose one or more services.** Some buyers place backorders with several services to improve their chances, accepting that each may charge if it wins.
4. **Set your maximum.** Decide the most you will pay if the name goes to auction.
5. **Wait, and plan for failure.** Keep a backup name in mind in case the owner renews or another buyer wins.

### Domain backorder vs buying the name today

A domain backorder is a bet on a name that may never drop. Buying a name that is already for sale replaces that uncertainty with a known price and a known timeline.

| Factor | Domain backorder | Buying a listed name |
|---|---|---|
| Certainty | Low; depends on the owner not renewing | High once payment and transfer complete |
| Timeline | Weeks to months, and may never happen | Usually days, depending on the transfer |
| Price | Low fee if uncontested, auction if not | Known upfront, or agreed by offer |
| Choice | Limited to names that happen to expire | Whatever is listed for sale |

## How to stop your own domains from expiring

The same lifecycle works against you if you forget a renewal. Owners lose valuable names this way every year, often because a card expired or reminder emails went to an old address. A few habits prevent it.

- **Turn on auto-renew** for every name you want to keep.
- **Keep your account email current,** and use an address on a different domain from the one that might expire.
- **Update your payment card** before it expires.
- **Renew important names for several years** at a time.
- **Keep a list** of every name you own, its registrar and its expiry date.

If a name has already expired, contact your registrar at once. During the grace period, renewal is usually simple. During redemption, recovery usually costs more but is still possible. Once a name reaches pending delete, it is out of your hands.
