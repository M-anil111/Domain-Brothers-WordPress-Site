Domain extensions are the part of the address after the dot, and the extension is the first thing a reader judges. It tells them what kind of business you are, where you are and how established you seem. This guide compares the main families and shows how to choose. It is part of our [complete guide to premium domain names](/premium-domain-names-complete-guide/).

!takeaways In short
- .com is the safe default for any business that may grow beyond one country.
- A country code can match or beat .com for a business that serves one country.
- Technology extensions suit start-ups but may cost much more to renew.
- Whatever you pick, secure the obvious alternatives and redirect them.

## What domain extensions are

Every domain ends in a top-level domain, or TLD. The [IANA root zone database](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db) lists all of them and the organisation that runs each one, and the [Wikipedia article on top-level domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top-level_domain) explains the families: generic ones such as .com and .org, country codes such as .ca and .de, and the newer extensions introduced since 2013.

## Which domain extensions fit which business

!img:infographic-i3|Infographic comparing domain extensions: .com for global trust, country codes for local trust, .io .ai and .co for technology start-ups, and new descriptive extensions|The main extension families and who they suit.

| Extension family | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| .com | Any business that sells widely or may go global | Short names cost the most because demand is highest |
| Country codes (.ca, .co.uk, .de, .in) | Businesses that serve one country | Some have residency rules, and some are less known abroad |
| Technology (.io, .ai, .co) | Software, start-ups, developer tools | High renewal prices and registry rule changes |
| New descriptive (.shop, .app, .blog, .agency) | A clear niche where the extension adds meaning | Lower recognition and some email filters distrust unusual endings |
| .org, .net | Non-profits, communities, networks | Buyers often assume the .com is the "real" one |

## .com: the default, and why

People type .com without thinking. If your address ends in anything else, some of your visitors will land on a different site. It is also what most people assume when they hear a name spoken. For that reason, a good .com is the most valuable type of premium domain, and names on it cost more than the same word on any other extension. If your budget allows, and your business may grow, start here. For a business that sells in more than one country, .com is still the safest domain extension to start from.

## Country codes: the local advantage

Country-code domains tell customers you are local. A Canadian business with a .ca address says it serves Canada, and customers who prefer Canadian suppliers notice. Search engines also treat country codes as a signal for local relevance. Many registries, including the Canadian one, set rules about who may register, such as having a presence in the country, so check the rules before you buy. We list a good range of [.ca and other country-code names](/all-domains/) alongside .com names.

If you plan to sell abroad, think about owning both the country code and the .com, and redirecting one to the other.

## Technology extensions

Extensions such as .io, .ai and .co became popular with software companies and start-ups. They can look modern and fit the field. Before you buy, compare the **renewal price** (often far higher than the first-year price), the registry's rules, and how your investors and customers see the extension. A short name on a technology extension is not automatically cheaper than the same name on .com in the long run.

Several popular technology domain extensions are country codes in origin: .io belongs to the British Indian Ocean Territory, .ai to Anguilla and .co to Colombia. Their rules and prices follow the registry for that territory, which is worth knowing before you build a company on one.

## New descriptive extensions

Names such as `bakery.shop` or `lawfirm.agency` use the extension as part of the message. They work best when the word before the dot is strong, and when the extension makes the meaning clearer rather than vaguer. They are less likely to be typed from memory, so back them with strong branding and links. Before you commit, test the address in an email signature and say it on a phone call to hear whether people repeat it correctly.

## Renewal costs and registry rules

The purchase price is paid once; the renewal is paid every year for as long as you keep the name. Over ten years the renewals on some domain extensions add up to more than the first-year price, so look at both before you choose. Compare the renewal fee of each domain extension over at least five years, not only the first-year price.

### Why renewal prices differ between domain extensions

Each extension is run by a registry that sets the wholesale price, and your registrar adds its own margin. Prices for some long-established extensions, such as .com, are governed by the registry's agreement with [ICANN](https://www.icann.org/), which limits how fast they can rise. Many country codes and newer extensions can change prices with fewer limits, and some registries charge a higher renewal for names they class as premium. Ask your registrar for the current renewal price, not just the first-year offer.

### Registry rules to check before you buy

- **Residency.** Some country codes, including .ca, require a connection to the country, such as being a citizen, a resident or a registered business there.
- **Security.** Some extensions, such as .app and .dev, only work over HTTPS, so the site needs a valid certificate from day one.
- **Use restrictions.** A few extensions are limited to verified organisations in one sector.
- **Policy changes.** A registry can change its rules or prices over time, and country codes depend on the policy of the country behind them. Read the registry's own terms.

## Using more than one extension

Many businesses own several domain extensions and use only one as the main address. The others protect the brand and catch visitors who guess the wrong ending.

- **Pick one main address.** Put the website, email and marketing on a single domain so links and reputation build in one place.
- **Redirect the rest.** Send every other extension to the main address with a permanent (301) redirect, so visitors and search engines end up in the same place.
- **Country sites.** A business that sells in several countries can use a country code for each market, or one .com with a section per country. Separate country domains give the strongest local signal but mean more sites to run.
- **Changing later.** If you move from one extension to another, follow a proper migration plan. [Change Your Domain Name Without Losing SEO](/change-domain-name-without-losing-seo/) shows the steps.

## How to decide in five questions

1. **Where are your customers?** One country points to a country code; many countries point to .com.
2. **How will people hear your name?** If by voice, avoid confusion. Picking .com removes one thing to explain.
3. **What can you afford over five years?** Add the purchase price and the renewals.
4. **Who else holds the other extensions?** If a competitor has the .com, think about confusion and the cost of buying it later.
5. **Is the extension stable?** Check the registry's rules and history.

## Protect your name

Once you have chosen, think about the cheap protections. If a second domain extension matters to your brand, register it at the same time and redirect it to the main site.

- Register the other extensions you care about and redirect them to your main address.
- Register obvious misspellings if they are a real risk.
- Turn on the registrar lock and auto-renew.
- Renew the main domain for several years at once, so one failed card payment never puts it at risk.

## Domain extensions and SEO

Google has said that the extension is not a ranking factor on its own in most cases, apart from country codes being used for geotargeting. A country code gives a clear local signal; a generic extension needs the geotargeting to come from content and settings. If you serve several countries, Google Search Central's guide to [managing multi-regional and multilingual sites](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites) compares country-code domains with subdirectories and subdomains.
