Knowing how to buy a domain name for a website saves you money and weeks of delay, because the right route depends on one question: does somebody already own the name? If nobody does, you register it in minutes. If somebody does, you buy it from them, agree a price, pay safely and move it into your own account. This guide covers both routes from the first idea to a live site, and links to a deeper article wherever a step has its own complications.

!takeaways In short
- Decide what the name must do for your business before you search, so you judge every option against the same test.
- An unregistered name costs a yearly registrar fee; a taken or listed premium name costs whatever buyer and owner agree.
- Agree the price, payment method and transfer steps in writing before any money moves.
- Plan the launch before the transfer finishes: DNS, email, HTTPS and redirects all depend on the new name.

## Step 1: Decide what the name must do

!img:ib-pillar-a|How to buy a domain name for a website. Two ways to get a domain name: Register a new name, Buy a taken name|Two ways to get a domain name.

Every guide on how to buy a domain name for a website should start here, because the search is easier when you know what you are judging. Write down who the site is for, where those people live, and how they will hear the name: in an ad, a podcast, a referral or a search result.

Then set a few rules for the name itself:

- **Length.** Shorter names are easier to type, say and remember. Our guide to [short domain names](/short-domain-names/) explains why two, three and four character names are scarce.
- **Meaning.** A descriptive name tells people what you do. A brandable name is invented or borrowed and gives you a distinct identity; see [brandable domain names](/brandable-domain-names/).
- **Extension.** .com is still the default for most audiences. A country code can serve a local market better. Our [guide to choosing an extension](/choosing-a-domain-extension-com-ca-io-and-more/) compares the main options.
- **Business fit.** A company name, a product name and a campaign name have different jobs. Read [domain names for business](/domain-names-for-business/) before you commit to one.

Make a shortlist of five to ten candidates before you look at prices; learning how to buy a domain name for a website is much easier with options in hand. Say each one aloud, spell it to a friend, and check that nobody already trades under it in your market.

## Step 2: Check who holds the name you want

The second step in how to buy a domain name for a website is to type each candidate into any registrar's search box. The result falls into one of three groups: available to register, registered but listed for sale, or registered and not listed.

For a registered name, look up the registration record. The [ICANN Lookup tool](https://lookup.icann.org/en) queries the public RDAP and WHOIS data and shows the registrar, the creation date and the expiry date. Many owners use privacy protection, so the record often hides the owner's name and offers a relay email address or contact form instead.

The expiry date matters. A name close to expiry may drop back into the pool if the owner does not renew it. Our guide to [domain backorders and expired domains](/domain-backorder-and-expired-domains/) explains the typical deletion cycle and how backorder services try to catch a dropping name.

## How to buy a domain name for a website that nobody owns

If the search says the name is available, how to purchase a domain name is simple: the purchase is a registration, not a sale. This is the only part of how to buy a domain name for a website that needs no negotiation. You pay a registrar, a company accredited to sell names under each extension, and you hold the name for the term you pay for, usually one to ten years.

Follow these steps:

1. Pick a reputable registrar and create an account with an email address you will keep for years.
2. Add the name to the cart and choose the registration term.
3. Turn on WHOIS privacy if the registrar offers it, and turn on auto-renew.
4. Check the renewal price as well as the first-year price. Some registries mark certain unregistered names as premium and charge more to register or renew them.
5. Pay, then confirm the registrant email the registrar sends you. Unconfirmed contact details can lead to suspension.

Registration takes minutes, which makes this the quickest answer to how to buy a domain name for a website. The name is yours for as long as you keep renewing it, so a lapsed card or an old email address is the main risk at this stage.

## How to buy a domain name for a website that is already taken

Most short, clear names in .com have been registered for years. If your first choice is taken, you have three options: buy it, choose another name, or wait in case it expires. Buying is often the cheapest of the three once you count the cost of a weaker brand. This section covers that route.

### How to buy a domain name for a website from a listing

Some owners list their names for sale with a fixed price or an invitation to offer. On our site you can browse [listed premium names](/all-domains/), pay the listed price, or send an offer. A broker replies to every offer within one business day, and the conversation continues until both sides agree or one side walks away.

A listed name is the simplest premium domain purchase, because the owner has already decided to sell. Your job is to judge the price, confirm the seller controls the name, and agree the transfer steps.

### Buying a name the owner has not listed

An unlisted name needs an approach. You find the owner, make contact, value the name and negotiate. Our guide on [how to buy a domain name from someone](/how-to-buy-a-domain-name-from-someone/) covers each of those steps, including what to say in the first message.

Many buyers hand this work to a broker. Our [domain brokerage service](/domain-brokerage-services/) finds and buys names that someone else owns, confidentially. We write down the research first (ownership, comparable sales and a price range) before anyone is contacted, and we confirm the terms in writing before you pay.

## Step 3: Price and value when you buy a domain name for a website

Registration fees are set by registrars and are broadly similar for the same extension. Aftermarket prices are not. Two buyers can value the same name very differently, because it is worth different things to each of them.

| Route | What you pay | What decides the cost |
|---|---|---|
| Register an available name | Yearly registrar fee | The extension and the registrar |
| Buy a listed premium name | The listed price or an agreed offer | Length, meaning, extension and comparable sales |
| Buy an unlisted name | A negotiated price, plus broker fees if you use one | The owner's plans and how much the name matters to you |

Price is where most questions about how to buy a domain name for a website end up. Before you make an offer, read [how to value a premium domain name](/how-to-value-a-premium-domain-name/) and check recorded sales on our [sold domains](/sold/) page.

Remember who is on the other side. Some owners are businesses that once used the name. Others are investors who buy names to resell; our guide to [domain flipping](/domain-flipping/) explains how they think about price. A name showing a parked page with ads is usually held by an investor, and our [domain parking](/domain-parking/) guide explains what that page tells you.

## Step 4: Pay safely

Part of knowing how to buy a domain name for a website is knowing how money and control of the name move, and in what order. Agree the price, the payment method, who starts the transfer and the deadline in writing. Never pay a stranger by a method you cannot trace.

Common ways to pay:

- **Card payment in full.** On our site card payments run through Stripe.
- **Payment plan.** We offer monthly plans of 3 to 12 months. The 3-month plan adds no interest; 6-month plans add 5%, 9-month plans add 8% and 12-month plans add 10%. Details are in [premium domain payment plans and financing](/premium-domain-payment-plans-and-financing/).
- **Escrow.** In private sales between strangers, a neutral escrow company can hold the money until the name arrives. Read [domain escrow explained](/domain-escrow-explained/) for how it works. Domain Brothers does not offer escrow.
- **Lease or lease-to-own.** Some sellers rent a name or let you pay over time while they keep ownership. See [domain leasing and lease-to-own](/domain-leasing-and-lease-to-own/) to compare it with a payment plan.

All domain sales on our site are final. When you work out how to buy a domain name for a website on a plan, compare the total cost with the full price, and check the spelling, the extension and the price before you pay.

## Step 5: Transfer the name into your account

A sold name moves in one of two ways. The seller either pushes it to your account at the same registrar, or gives you a transfer (EPP) code so you can move it to your own registrar. Our [domain transfer](/domain-transfer/) page explains how we handle this, and our [EPP guidelines](/epp-guidelines/) cover the code itself.

Expect a few rules. Under ICANN's [Transfer Policy](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/transfer-policy-2016-06-01-en), registrars typically lock a name against moving to another registrar for 60 days after a registration, a previous transfer or a change of registrant. That lock does not stop you from using the name; it only delays a registrar change.

Transfer codes you submit on our site are encrypted and erased after 30 days. Once the name shows in your account, the ownership part of how to buy a domain name for a website is done: update the contact details, turn on auto-renew and enable the registrar lock.
