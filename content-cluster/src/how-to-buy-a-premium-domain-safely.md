Premium domains are valuable and easy to move, which makes them attractive to fraudsters. The good news is that a handful of habits remove almost all of the risk. Follow this process and you can buy with confidence from a marketplace, a broker or a private seller. It is part of our [complete guide to premium domain names](/premium-domain-names-complete-guide/).

!takeaways In short
- Check the name and the seller before you talk price.
- Pay through escrow, never straight to an individual.
- Put the agreement in writing, including what is transferred and when.
- When the name arrives, lock it, secure the account and keep your records.

## Stage 1: Find the right name

Start with what you need the name to do. Write down three to five candidates, then search by keyword, length or category. ## Stage 2: Check the name and the seller

### The name

- **Trademarks.** Search the trademark register of each market you will sell in. In the United States, start at the [USPTO trademark search](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks). A match can mean losing the name later.
- **History.** Look the name up on a web archive to see what it was used for. Avoid names with a spam, adult or scam past.
- **Spelling and sound.** Say it aloud and spell it to a friend.
- **Renewal price.** Some extensions charge much more to renew than to register.

### The seller

- Ask the seller to prove control, for example by changing a harmless text record at the DNS or by logging in at the registrar while you watch on a call.
- If you are dealing with a broker, ask who they represent and ask for the agreement.
- Check the seller's history, reviews and business details. We have been selling premium domains for many years, and you can [read about us](/about-domain-brothers/) and meet [our team](/our-team/).

## Stage 3: Agree the price and the terms

You will meet two kinds of listing. Some have a **fixed price** you can pay at once. Others are **open to offers**, where you propose a price and the seller replies. On our site, every offer is read by a person and answered, and each conversation stays on a private page so nothing is lost.

Agree these points in writing before money moves.

1. The exact domain name, with spelling.
2. The price and the currency.
3. Who pays the escrow fee.
4. The method of transfer and the registrar involved.
5. The date by which the transfer will be complete.
6. What happens if the transfer fails, including a full refund.

## Stage 4: Pay through escrow

Escrow means a neutral third party holds your money until the seller delivers the name. The seller is told that the money is safe, so they start the transfer. When you confirm that the name is yours, the escrow agent releases the funds. [Escrow.com](https://www.escrow.com/) is the best known service, and its [introduction to escrow](https://www.escrow.com/what-is-escrow) is a good place to see the steps.

!callout Never do this|Do not send a wire, crypto or gift card payment straight to a stranger. Do not move the name first on a promise to pay. Do not accept a "transfer" that is only a login to an account. If the seller refuses escrow, treat it as a red flag and walk away.

If you are buying from us, payments are processed securely and the transfer is protected by escrow, so you and the name are both protected during the process.

## Stage 5: Transfer, lock and secure

### How the transfer works

For most names, the seller unlocks the domain at their registrar and gives you an **authorisation code** (often called an EPP code). You start a transfer at your registrar with the code, and the old registrar releases the domain. The name then sits in your account. The details are in [Domain Transfers, EPP Codes and DNS Explained](/domain-transfers-epp-codes-and-dns-explained/) and our [EPP guidelines](/epp-guidelines/). The [ICANN transfer policy](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/transfer-policy-2016-06-01-en) sets the rules registrars follow.

### Secure it the same day

- Turn on the registrar lock.
- Turn on two-factor sign-in on the registrar account.
- Set auto-renew and check the contact email addresses.
- Save the receipt, the agreement and the transfer confirmation in a safe place.

## Red flags that should stop you

- Pressure to pay today or lose the name.
- A new email address that does not match the company.
- A price far below similar names with no reason.
- No escrow, or a fake escrow link on a site you do not recognise.
- A seller who cannot show control of the domain.
- A name that matches a famous brand.
