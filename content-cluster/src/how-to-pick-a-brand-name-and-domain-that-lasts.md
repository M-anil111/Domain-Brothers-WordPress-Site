Knowing how to choose a domain name matters because a name is the most expensive thing to change after you launch. It is on your site, your invoices, your shopfront and your customers' lips. This guide shows how to choose a name that is easy to say, easy to find and easy to grow into, and how to match it to a domain you can actually own. It is part of our [complete guide to premium domain names](/premium-domain-names-complete-guide/).

!takeaways In short
- Choose the name and the domain together, not one after the other.
- Run four quick tests: say it, spell it, search it, picture it.
- Avoid hyphens, odd spellings and names that tie you to one product.
- Check trademarks before you fall in love with a name.

## What a good name does

A good name has five jobs.

1. It is **easy to remember** after one hearing.
2. It is **easy to say and spell**, so word of mouth works.
3. It is **different enough** from competitors to stand out.
4. It **suggests something** about what you do or how you feel, without trapping you.
5. It is **available**: as a domain, on social media, and as a trademark in your markets.

The web address is not a separate decision. Customers will find you by it, so the name and the domain have to fit. Our [guide to valuing premium domains](/how-to-value-a-premium-domain-name/) shows how names are rated in the market, and many of the same traits make a name a good brand.

## Four types of brand name

| Type | Example idea | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | Says what you do | Instantly clear | Hard to own, easy to copy |
| Suggestive | Hints at a benefit | Memorable and meaningful | Needs a little explaining |
| Invented or blended | A coined word | Easy to protect | Needs marketing to give it meaning |
| Real word, new use | An ordinary word applied to your field | Easy to recall and often strong | May be taken as a domain, so costs more |

Many premium domains fall in the last two groups, which is why they cost more: they are the names that brands are built on.

When you work out how to choose a domain name for your own business, decide first which type you want, because it changes where you search: descriptive names point to keyword domains, invented names to short brandable ones, and real-word names to the premium aftermarket.

## The four-test method

Run each candidate through these.

1. **The phone test.** Say the name to a friend and ask them to write it down. If they get it wrong, you will lose customers the same way.
2. **The radio test.** Imagine hearing it once on the radio. Could you type the address? Names with hyphens, numbers or creative spellings fail.
3. **The search test.** Search the name. Look at what already appears. A crowded result page is a warning.
4. **The picture test.** Imagine it on a sign, a van, an app icon and an email address. Does it look good in each?

## Naming mistakes that force a rename

- **Tying the name to one product.** A name that is perfect today can be wrong in three years.
- **Hyphens and numbers.** People forget them, and they hurt recall.
- **Clever spellings.** They look fun and cost you visitors who type the usual spelling.
- **Almost-a-famous-brand.** It invites a legal letter and confuses customers.
- **Names that are hard in another language.** Check the sound and meaning in the languages you will sell in.
- **Settling for a long domain.** If the best name is only available as a long address, the name is not available.

## How to choose a domain name to match your brand

1. **List your top ten names** and check each at once for domain, social handles and trademarks.
2. **Look at the domain tier.** Is the .com owned? Is it for sale? Our [domain list](/all-domains/) and [checker](/check-domain/) help.
3. **Decide how much the name is worth to you.** A single-word or short brandable name is worth the price if it will carry the business.
4. **Consider alternatives:** a country code, a close variation, or a different word with the same meaning. [Choosing a Domain Extension](/choosing-a-domain-extension-com-ca-io-and-more/) compares them.
5. **Secure everything together.** When you commit, buy the domain, claim the social handles and file the trademark if appropriate. We also help with [social media handles](/acquire-social-media-handles/).

## How to choose a domain name when your first choice is taken

Most good names are already registered. That is a reason to look harder, not to settle for a long or awkward address.

### Find out whether the domain is for sale

A registered name is not always an unavailable one. Search our [domain list](/all-domains/) and other marketplaces first. If the name is not listed, our [brokerage service](/domain-brokerage-services/) can approach the owner confidentially, so your interest does not push the price up. If the name looks unused and close to expiry, [Domain Backorders and Expired Domains](/domain-backorder-and-expired-domains/) explains how expiring names come back to the market.

### Add a word, carefully

A short prefix or suffix such as "get", "try" or "hq" can turn a taken name into an available one. It works when the core word stays clear and the addition is easy to say. It fails when customers drop the extra word and land on someone else's site, so check who owns the plain version first.

### Change the name or the extension

If the plain .com belongs to an active business in your field, a close variation will send you their customers' confusion and possibly their lawyer's letter. A country code can work for a business that serves one country, and a different name altogether is often the better long-term answer.

## Length, characters and spelling

Every extra character is another chance for a typo. Aim for a domain that is short enough to read at a glance; under about fifteen characters is a sensible target, and shorter is better.

### How to choose a domain name length that works

- Prefer one or two real words over a phrase.
- Leave out hyphens and numbers unless they are part of the brand itself.
- Watch for doubled letters where two words meet, which people often mistype.
- Avoid plurals that change the meaning or that people will forget.
- Read the joined-up words in lowercase to make sure they do not spell something unintended.

Our guides to [short domain names](/short-domain-names/) and [brandable domain names](/brandable-domain-names/) go deeper on both kinds.

## Check the legal side early

Search the trademark register in your market before you commit. In the United States the [USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks) has a free search. A conflict can cost you the name later, even if you own the domain. For other markets, the [WIPO Global Brand Database](https://branddb.wipo.int/) searches trademarks registered in many countries at once, and a local trademark lawyer can confirm the result before you file. Our article on [trademarks and domain disputes](/trademarks-and-domain-disputes-udrp-basics/) explains the main rules.

## The address is part of the interface

The [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/url-as-ui/) points out that people read the address as part of the user interface, and the [W3C's guidance on URIs](https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI) says good addresses are simple, stable and readable. Choose a name that follows those principles and your site is easier to share. In practice that means one clear address, written the same way everywhere, with every other version of the name redirecting to it.

## A short naming workshop you can run in an afternoon

This workshop puts the whole method on how to choose a domain name into one afternoon.

1. Write down what you do, who it is for and three feelings you want customers to have.
2. Brainstorm fifty names without judging them.
3. Cut the list to ten using the four tests.
4. Check domains, handles and trademarks for the ten.
5. Ask five people from your target audience to say, spell and describe each remaining name.
6. Choose, buy the domain and move.
