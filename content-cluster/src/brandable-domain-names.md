Brandable domain names work as a brand on their own: short, easy to say, easy to spell and free of a literal meaning that could box you in. Many young companies choose one because it can grow with the business. This guide covers patterns, testing and checks, and is part of our guide on [how to buy a domain name for a website](/how-to-buy-a-domain-name-for-a-website/).

!takeaways In short
- A brandable domain is easy to say, spell and remember, and leaves room for the business to grow.
- Most brandable domain names follow a few patterns: invented words, blends, real words used in a new way, and short compounds.
- Test a shortlist on real people with a radio test and a recall test before you commit.
- Run trademark and history checks first, because a brand you cannot protect is not much of a brand.

## What makes brandable domain names work?

!img:ib-brandable-a|Common patterns in brandable domain names: Real word, Compound, Blend, Invented, Suffix, Metaphor|Common patterns in brandable domain names.


A brand name does a different job from a descriptive name. A descriptive name says what you sell. A brand name gives customers something distinct to remember and to search for later. The best brandable domain names share a handful of traits.

- **Short.** Fewer syllables are easier to remember and to type. Most strong brandable domains have two or three syllables.
- **Easy to say.** If people hesitate over the pronunciation, they hesitate to recommend you.
- **Easy to spell after hearing it once.** This is the "radio test", covered below.
- **Distinct.** It should not sound like a competitor or a famous company.
- **Open-ended.** It should not lock the business into one product, city or year.
- **Positive or neutral connotations** in the languages your customers speak.

The [Wikipedia article on brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand) explains why a distinct name matters: it is the anchor customers attach your reputation to.

### Brandable domain names vs keyword domains

Neither type is better in every case. A keyword domain tells people what you do on first sight. A brandable domain gives you a name you can own, protect and stretch into new products.

| Factor | Brandable domain | Keyword domain |
|---|---|---|
| Says what you do | Not by itself | Yes |
| Trademark protection | Usually easier for distinct names | Harder for generic descriptive words |
| Room to grow | Wide | Tied to the words in the name |
| Memorability | High if short and pleasant | High if short and common |
| Price on the aftermarket | Varies widely | Short generic words are often costly |

## Common patterns behind brandable domain names

Most brandable domain names fall into a few recognisable patterns. The examples below are invented to show the pattern; they are not claims about any real company.

1. **Invented words.** Pleasant-sounding words with no fixed meaning, such as "Velora" or "Truvo". They are distinct and often easier to protect, but you have to teach people what they mean.
2. **Blends.** Two words or word parts joined into one, such as "Brightlo" from "bright" and "flow". They hint at meaning while staying unique.
3. **Real words used in a new context.** An everyday word applied to an unrelated product, like a fruit name for a software tool. These are memorable but often contested.
4. **Short compounds.** Two short real words joined, such as "Peakwell" or "Fieldhouse". They read naturally and suggest a feeling.
5. **Altered spellings.** A real word with a dropped vowel or swapped letter. Use these carefully, because they often fail the radio test.
6. **Very short names.** Three to five characters, including letter combinations. See our [short domain names](/short-domain-names/) guide.

### Which brandable domain names pattern suits your business?

Consumer brands often benefit from warm, easy-to-say invented words or compounds. Business-to-business firms may prefer short compounds that hint at reliability or expertise. Whatever you choose, the name should sound right when a customer says it to a friend.

## How to build a shortlist of brandable domains

Good brandable domain names rarely arrive fully formed. Start with words, not domains. Write down what you want customers to feel about the business, the problem you solve, and a few words from your industry. Then combine, shorten and reshape them using the patterns above.

- **Aim for quantity first.** A long list of rough ideas gives you more good options than a short list of polished ones.
- **Cut hard.** Remove anything longer than three syllables, anything hard to spell and anything close to a competitor.
- **Check availability early.** Look up the .com for each survivor before anyone grows attached to a name.
- **Keep five to ten finalists.** That is enough to test properly without tiring the people you ask.

Expect many favourites to be registered already. That is normal. A registered name may still be for sale, either listed on a marketplace or through its owner.

## How to test brandable domains before you buy

A name that looks good on a shortlist can fail in conversation. Testing takes an afternoon and saves you from an expensive mistake. Ask people outside your team, ideally people who resemble your customers.

1. **The radio test.** Say the name aloud once to someone, then ask them to type it. If many people misspell it, you will lose visitors and email.
2. **The recall test.** Mention three or four candidate names in conversation. A day later, ask which ones they remember.
3. **The meaning test.** Ask what kind of business they think each name belongs to. You want answers that fit, or at least do not clash.
4. **The language check.** Look up the name in the main languages of your markets for unwanted meanings or slang.
5. **The visual test.** Write the name in lower case and capitals, and look for awkward letter combinations or accidental words.

Also check how the name looks as a handle on the social platforms you plan to use. Consistent handles make brandable domain names easier to find and to recommend.

## Trademark and history checks for a brand name domain

A brand is only valuable if you can keep it. Before you buy a brand name domain, run these checks.

- **Trademark search.** Search official databases in each country where you will trade. In the United States, start with the [USPTO trademark search](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search). Look for identical and similar-sounding marks in related goods and services.
- **Company and app names.** Search business registers, app stores and the web for companies already using the name in your field.
- **Domain history.** Check old versions of the domain in a web archive for spam, adult content or other baggage.
- **Extension variations.** See who owns the same name in other common extensions, and whether that could confuse customers.

A trademark lawyer can give you a clearance opinion for a serious launch. Our guide to [trademarks and domain disputes](/trademarks-and-domain-disputes-udrp-basics/) explains what happens when names conflict.

## Mistakes to avoid with a brand name domain

A few errors come up again and again when businesses choose brandable domains.

- **Choosing a name only the founders can spell.** If you have to explain the spelling every time, the name is costing you.
- **Copying a famous brand's sound.** A name that echoes a well-known company invites confusion and possibly a legal dispute.
- **Adding hyphens or numbers to make the name fit.** These are hard to say aloud and easy to forget.
- **Picking a trend word.** Words tied to a passing fashion can date the business quickly.
- **Skipping the checks to save time.** Renaming after launch costs far more than a week of testing.

If you already run under a weaker name, moving is possible with planning. Our guide on how to [change a domain name without losing SEO](/change-domain-name-without-losing-seo/) explains the steps.

## Choosing an extension for brandable domains

The extension matters as much as the name in front of it. A .com is still the default that most people type and remember worldwide. A country code can be a strong choice for a business that serves one country. Newer extensions can work for a startup audience, but customers may still type .com out of habit.

If the .com of your favourite brandable name belongs to someone else, think carefully before building a brand on a different extension. Our guide to [choosing a domain extension](/choosing-a-domain-extension-com-ca-io-and-more/) compares the main options.
