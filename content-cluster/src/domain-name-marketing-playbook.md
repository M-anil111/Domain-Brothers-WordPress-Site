A premium domain is only as valuable as the marketing built on it. The good news is that a short, clear name makes every channel a little cheaper and every message a little easier to remember. This playbook shows where and how to use the name, with a 30 day plan to launch it. It is part of our [complete guide to premium domain names](/premium-domain-names-complete-guide/).

!takeaways In short
- Put the name on every touchpoint: email, ads, print, social profiles and packaging.
- Use short, memorable addresses for campaigns so people can type them from memory.
- Measure everything with tagged links and conversion goals.
- Follow a plan, so the name starts working in the first month.

## 1. Branded email

Every email you send carries your domain. A professional address builds trust before the message is read, and it is the cheapest advertising you will ever do.

- Use real names or roles: `name@yourdomain`, `hello@`, `sales@`.
- Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC so messages reach inboxes. See our [launch checklist](/website-launch-checklist-after-buying-a-domain/) and the [DMARC overview](https://dmarc.org/overview/).
- Add a short signature with your name, role, phone and website.

## 2. Paid advertising

A short, readable domain looks better in the ad's display URL and is easier to remember after one view.

- Use the name in headlines and display URLs where the platform allows.
- Send ad traffic to dedicated landing pages on the same domain, so the brand builds with every click.
- Track results with campaign tags on every link.
- Use branded search ads to protect your own name from competitors.

Our [digital marketing services](/digital-marketing-services/) team runs and manages campaigns if you want help.

## 3. Print, QR codes and events

Short names are easy to read from a slide or a sign.

- Put the domain, not a long link, on business cards, packaging, vehicles and banners.
- For a campaign, use a QR code that points to a short, memorable page on your domain, and test it on several phones.
- Repeat the address aloud in talks and videos; short names make this natural.

## 4. Social profiles and handles

Use the same name on every platform. If the name is not available everywhere, keep the closest consistent version, and link each profile to the website. We can help you [acquire social media handles](/acquire-social-media-handles/) that match your domain.

## 5. Vanity URLs and campaign pages

A vanity URL is a short, memorable address that forwards to a longer page, such as `yourdomain/spring` or a campaign on a short second name.

- Choose words people can say and spell.
- Always forward with a permanent or temporary redirect, as fits the campaign.
- Tag the destination so you can see which channel brought each visitor.

## 6. Search and content

A great domain does not rank by itself, but it helps in the ways that matter: people click it, remember it and search for it by name. Pair it with helpful content. Read [Do Premium Domains Help SEO?](/do-premium-domains-help-seo/) for the details, and follow Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) for the basics.

## 7. Word of mouth

The ultimate test is whether a customer can hear your name once and type it correctly. If the answer is yes, every happy customer becomes a marketing channel. Keep the name easy to say, and ask for referrals in your emails and receipts.

## A 30 day plan to launch a new name

**Week 1: Foundation**
1. Secure the domain, connect DNS and turn on HTTPS.
2. Set up branded email with authentication.
3. Publish the home, about and contact pages.
4. Add analytics and Search Console.

**Week 2: Presence**
1. Claim matching social handles and complete each profile.
2. Update your email signature and every place the old name appears.
3. Tell your customer list about the new address.
4. Submit your sitemap and check indexing.

**Week 3: Campaigns**
1. Create one landing page for each main offer.
2. Launch a small paid campaign to the landing pages.
3. Print or share a QR code at one event or in one mailing.
4. Reach out to partners for links and mentions.

**Week 4: Measure and improve**
1. Review conversions by channel.
2. Fix the weakest page and double the best channel.
3. Publish a helpful article that answers a common question.
4. Plan the next month.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Changing the name between campaigns.
- Using long, hard-to-type links in print.
- Sending ad traffic to the home page instead of a focused page.
- Forgetting to authenticate email.
- Not tracking where leads come from.
