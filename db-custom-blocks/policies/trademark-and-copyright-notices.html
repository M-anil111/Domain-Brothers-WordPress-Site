1. Overview

Domain names are registered on a first-come basis, and some names resemble brands. We respect the rights of trademark and copyright owners. This page explains how to send us a notice and what we do with it. Buyers are responsible for their own checks, as our Terms of Use explain.

2. Trademark complaints about a listed domain

If you own a trademark and believe a domain we list infringes it, email sales@domainbrothers.com with the subject "Trademark notice" and include:

  • the domain name and the page address of the listing;
  • your name, your company and your contact details;
  • the trademark, its registration number, the country or region, and a copy of the registration;
  • why you believe the domain infringes your rights;
  • a statement that the information in your notice is accurate, and that you are the owner or authorised to act for the owner.

3. What we do

We review each notice and may remove or pause the listing while we look into it, contact the owner of the name, and ask you for more information. We may also tell the other party who sent the notice. We cannot decide who has the better right to a name. That is for the parties, a registry's dispute process or a court.

4. Domain disputes (UDRP)

Domain names are subject to the Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy (UDRP) and related registry processes. If a complaint is filed against a name that is still in our custody, we will cooperate with the process and follow its decision. A buyer takes a name subject to those processes, and a decision against a name is not a ground for a refund.

5. Copyright

If you believe content on our site copies your work, email sales@domainbrothers.com with the subject "Copyright notice". Include where the material appears, what the original work is and where it is published, your contact details, and a statement that you are the owner or are authorised to act for the owner and that your information is accurate. We forward copyright notices to the person responsible, in line with Canadian law, and may remove material that infringes.

6. Misuse of this process

Sending a notice you know to be false, or using it to harass, is misuse. We may ignore it and may take action.
