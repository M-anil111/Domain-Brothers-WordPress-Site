Buying premium domains carries one real risk: the names are valuable and easy to move, which makes them attractive to fraudsters. The good news is that a handful of habits remove almost all of that risk. Follow this process and buying premium domains from a marketplace, a broker or a private seller becomes routine. It is part of our complete guide to premium domain names.

In short

  • Check the name and the seller before you talk price.
  • When you buy from an individual you do not know, pay through escrow, never straight to them.
  • Put the agreement in writing, including what is transferred and when.
  • When the name arrives, lock it, secure the account and keep your records.

Buying premium domains: the five stages at a glance

Stage 1: Find the right name

Buying premium domains starts with what you need the name to do. Write down three to five candidates, then search by keyword, length or category. Our domain list is searchable, and you can browse by theme: business, technology, finance and legal, health and fitness, shopping and more. Use our domain checker to see whether a name is available at the registry or already owned. When buying premium domains, start with a shortlist of three to five names so you can walk away from any single one.

Stage 2: Check the name and the seller

The name

  • Trademarks. Search the trademark register of each market you will sell in. In the United States, start at the USPTO trademark search. A match can mean losing the name later.
  • History. Look the name up on a web archive to see what it was used for. Avoid names with a spam, adult or scam past.
  • Spelling and sound. Say it aloud and spell it to a friend.
  • Renewal price. Some extensions charge much more to renew than to register.
  • Registration record. Look up the name in a public RDAP or WHOIS directory to see the registrar, the creation date and the expiry date. A name close to expiry needs renewing as part of the deal.

The seller

  • Ask the seller to prove control, for example by changing a harmless text record at the DNS or by logging in at the registrar while you watch on a call.
  • If you are dealing with a broker, ask who they represent and ask for the agreement.
  • Check the seller's history, reviews and business details. You can read about us and meet our team.

Stage 3: Agree the price and the terms

You will meet two kinds of listing. Some have a fixed price you can pay at once. Others are open to offers, where you propose a price and the seller replies. On our site, every offer is read by a person and answered, and each conversation stays on a private page so nothing is lost. Put every term in writing: buying premium domains on a verbal promise is how most disputes start.

Agree these points in writing before money moves.

  1. The exact domain name, with spelling.
  2. The price and the currency.
  3. Who pays the escrow fee.
  4. The method of transfer and the registrar involved.
  5. The date by which the transfer will be complete.
  6. What happens if the transfer fails, including a full refund.

Stage 4: Pay through escrow

Escrow means a neutral third party holds your money until the seller delivers the name. The seller is told that the money is safe, so they start the transfer. When you confirm that the name is yours, the escrow agent releases the funds. Escrow.com is the best known service, and its introduction to escrow is a good place to see the steps.

Never do this

Do not send a wire, crypto or gift card payment straight to a stranger. Do not move the name first on a promise to pay. Do not accept a "transfer" that is only a login to an account. If a private seller you do not know refuses escrow, treat it as a red flag and walk away.

Buying from an established marketplace or broker is different: Domain Brothers, for example, does not use escrow, but sets out in writing how payment and transfer work before you pay, confirms your payment before the transfer starts, and answers any question first.

Stage 5: Transfer, lock and secure

The last stage of buying premium domains is the one most often rushed, so lock the name and turn on two-factor sign-in the day it arrives.

How the transfer works

For most names, the seller unlocks the domain at their registrar and gives you an authorisation code (often called an EPP code). You start a transfer at your registrar with the code, and the old registrar releases the domain. The name then sits in your account. The details are in Domain Transfers, EPP Codes and DNS Explained and our EPP guidelines. The ICANN transfer policy sets the rules registrars follow.

Secure it the same day

  • Turn on the registrar lock.
  • Turn on two-factor sign-in on the registrar account.
  • Set auto-renew and check the contact email addresses.
  • Save the receipt, the agreement and the transfer confirmation in a safe place.
  • Point the DNS at your host and set up email before you announce the new address.

When buying premium domains for a company, register the name to the company rather than to one employee, and give at least two trusted people access to the registrar account. That way the name never depends on one person's inbox.

Who you buy from changes the risk

The rules for buying premium domains depend on who is selling. The checks in Stage 2 apply to every purchase, but the payment step and the main risks differ.

Buying premium domains from a marketplace

With an established marketplace, most of your checks are about the marketplace itself: who runs it, how it confirms payment, and when the transfer starts. At Domain Brothers each listing shows a price you can pay now or invites an offer, a broker replies to offers within one business day, card payments run through Stripe, and the transfer starts once payment is confirmed. Because payment is confirmed before the name moves, there is no escrow step. All sales are final, so finish your checks on the name before you pay.

From a private owner you do not know

This is where most fraud happens. Make the seller prove control of the name, insist on escrow, and reach the escrow service by typing its address yourself rather than clicking a link the seller sends. Never wire money, send crypto or buy gift cards for a person you cannot identify, and ask whether the name is tied up in any dispute or unfinished agreement.

Through a broker

A broker can approach the owner of a name that is not openly for sale, keep your identity private and negotiate for you, which often stops the price rising when the owner learns who is asking. Ask who the broker represents, how they are paid and what the written agreement covers. Our brokerage service works confidentially for buyers, and How to Buy a Domain Name From Someone explains the general approach.

How long buying premium domains takes

Most of the time goes into choosing and checking, not the transfer itself.

  • Offer and reply. On our site a broker answers every offer within one business day. Negotiation with a private owner can take days or weeks.
  • Payment. A card payment in full is confirmed quickly. On a monthly plan of 3 to 12 months, the name stays with Domain Brothers until the final payment clears and then transfers to you.
  • Transfer. Many transfers finish within a few days once the name is unlocked and the authorisation code is in hand. Some registries add waiting periods, so allow up to a week.
  • Switching over. Updating email, signs, ads and profiles takes longer than the transfer. The Website Launch Checklist lists the order of work.

Set your launch date with that margin in mind, and do not print the new address on anything until the name sits in your own account.

Red flags when buying premium domains

Most fraud in buying premium domains starts with pressure to pay quickly or away from a protected payment route.

  • Pressure to pay today or lose the name.
  • A new email address that does not match the company.
  • A price far below similar names with no reason.
  • A private seller who refuses escrow, or a fake escrow link on a site you do not recognise.
  • A seller who cannot show control of the domain.
  • A name that matches a famous brand.

Frequently asked questions

Is buying premium domains safe?

Yes, when you check the name and the seller, agree the terms in writing and pay in a way that protects you. With an unknown private seller that means escrow. With an established marketplace that confirms payment before it starts the transfer, follow its written process and keep every receipt and message.

Do I need escrow when buying premium domains from Domain Brothers?

No. Domain Brothers does not use escrow for its own sales. Payment is confirmed before the transfer starts, the steps are set out before you pay, and a broker answers your questions first. Escrow remains good advice when you buy from a private seller you do not know.

Can I pay for a premium domain in instalments?

Yes. Domain Brothers takes card payment in full or on a monthly plan of 3 to 12 months. The three-month plan has no interest, six-month plans add 5%, nine-month plans add 8% and twelve-month plans add 10%. The name transfers after the final payment clears, and every instalment is non-refundable.

What should I do if a transfer is delayed?

Check that the name is unlocked, that the authorisation code is correct and that the contact email at the losing registrar works, because most delays come from one of those three. If you bought from a marketplace or broker, send them your order details so they can check the transfer status.
