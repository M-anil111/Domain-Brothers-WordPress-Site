Change domain name SEO work decides whether a rebrand keeps its search traffic or loses months of it. Search engines can follow a move from one domain to another, but only if every old address points clearly to its new home and the signals around the site agree. This guide is a practical checklist for a domain migration, from planning to the months after launch. Treat it as your change domain name SEO plan and work through it in order. It is part of our guide on how to buy a domain name for a website.

In short

  • Map every old URL to its closest new URL and use permanent 301 redirects, page to page, never all to the home page.
  • Verify both domains in Google Search Console and use the Change of Address tool once redirects are live.
  • Update sitemaps, internal links, canonical tags and the backlinks you can influence.
  • Expect some movement in rankings for a few weeks, keep the old domain registered and keep the redirects for the long term.

Why change domain name SEO planning matters

Your rankings are tied to URLs. Each page has earned links, history and trust at its current address. When you move to a new domain, that value only follows if search engines can see that the old page and the new page are the same thing.

A rebrand domain change done in a hurry often breaks this link. Common results are pages that return errors, redirects that all land on the home page, and a new site that blocks crawlers by mistake. Each of these can cost traffic that takes months to recover.

Google publishes guidance on site moves with URL changes. Read it alongside this checklist; it is the primary source on how Google treats a move.

Before you move: the change domain name SEO prep list

Good preparation does most of the change domain name SEO work. Do these things before any redirect goes live.

  1. Secure the new domain. Make sure it is registered in your own account, and check its history on a web archive for spam or past penalties.
  2. Crawl the old site. Export every URL that returns a page, including blog posts, product pages, PDFs and images that earn traffic.
  3. Pull your top pages and links. In Search Console and your analytics, list the pages with the most clicks and the most linking domains.
  4. Record a baseline. Save current clicks, impressions, indexed pages and rankings for your main terms, so you can compare later.
  5. Keep the structure stable if you can. Moving domain and redesigning at the same time makes problems harder to trace.

Build a 301 redirect map, page to page

The redirect map is the centre of any domain migration. Without it, no amount of other change domain name SEO effort can hold your rankings. It is a simple list: each old URL in one column, the matching new URL in the next.

A 301 redirect tells browsers and search engines that a page has moved permanently. Use it for every page in the map.

Change domain name SEO redirect rules

  • Redirect page to page. Sending every old URL to the new home page is treated much like removing the pages.
  • Avoid chains. Each old URL should reach its final new URL in one hop.
  • Keep query strings where they matter, such as product filters that earn traffic.
  • Test a sample of old URLs, plus your top 100 pages, before you announce the move.
  • Keep the redirects in place for at least a year, and longer if you can. Google's guidance recommends at least one year.

Launch day: Search Console and sitemaps

Once the new site is live and the redirects work, tell search engines about the move. Launch day is where the change domain name SEO plan meets real crawlers, so keep the team available to fix problems quickly.

  • Verify both domains in Google Search Console. You need ownership of the old and the new property.
  • Use the Change of Address tool in the old property. It tells Google the whole site has moved to the new domain. It is for moving a domain, not for switching from http to https.
  • Submit an XML sitemap for the new domain in the new property.
  • Check robots.txt on the new domain. A staging rule that blocks crawlers is one of the most common causes of a failed move.
  • Check noindex tags on the new pages, for the same reason.
  • Do the same in other search tools you use, such as Bing Webmaster Tools, which has its own site move feature.

Some teams also keep the old sitemap available for a short time, so crawlers revisit the old URLs and find the redirects faster. Remove it once the old URLs have dropped out of the index.

Update internal links, canonicals and other signals

Redirects catch old links, but your own site should not rely on them. Internal links are the part of change domain name SEO you fully control. Every internal signal should point straight to the new domain.

  • Replace old-domain internal links in menus, footers, body text and buttons.
  • Update canonical tags so each page names its own new URL.
  • Update hreflang tags if you run language or country versions.
  • Update structured data that includes your URL or logo address.
  • Change the URL in your analytics, tag manager, ad accounts and email platform.
  • Update your Google Business Profile, social profiles, app store listings and email signatures.

Backlinks outreach after a domain migration

Redirects pass visitors and signals from old links, but a direct link to the new domain is cleaner and does not depend on your redirects staying in place forever.

Outreach is the slowest part of change domain name SEO, so start it in the first week. Begin with your list of top linking domains. Contact the sites you have a relationship with first: partners, suppliers, directories, associations and press you have worked with. Send a short note with the exact old link and the new address.

You will not update every link, and that is fine. Focus on the links that send real visitors or come from well-known sites. If you previously uploaded a disavow file, upload it again in the new property.

Change domain name SEO monitoring after launch

Rankings often move for a while after a migration. How long depends on the size of the site and how often it is crawled. Watch for problems early so you can fix them while they are small.

First week

  • Crawl the old URL list and confirm each one returns a single 301 to the right page.
  • Watch the coverage and page indexing reports in both Search Console properties.
  • Check server logs or crawl stats to see that search engines are fetching the new domain.

First month

  • Compare clicks and impressions for your top pages against the baseline.
  • Look for 404 errors on the new domain and add missing redirects.
  • Confirm the new sitemap is processed and its pages are being indexed.

Months two to twelve

  • Track rankings for your main terms; most should settle as the move is processed.
  • Keep renewing the old domain. If it expires, every redirect stops and someone else can register it.
  • Review the redirect map once more before you make any further structure changes.

Does a new domain itself help or hurt rankings?

A new domain does not carry a ranking penalty just for being new, and a better name does not rank you by itself. What the move changes is the address that earned signals point to. Done well, a change domain name SEO plan carries those signals across.

A clearer, more memorable name can still help indirectly through higher click-through, more direct visits and easier word of mouth. We cover this in do premium domains help SEO. The benefit only shows if the migration itself is clean.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a domain migration take to recover?

It varies with the size of the site and how often search engines crawl it. Small sites often see most pages move within a few weeks, while large sites can take longer. Some ranking movement during that time is normal. Errors in redirects or blocked crawlers make recovery much slower, so test those first.

Should I keep the old domain after a rebrand domain change?

Yes. Keep the old domain registered and renewed for as long as it receives links or visits, which for most businesses means indefinitely. If it expires, all redirects stop working, old links break, and anyone can register the name and use it, including for spam that confuses your customers.

Can I redirect every old page to the new home page?

You can, but you should not. Google treats mass redirects to the home page much like removed pages, so the value of each old page is largely lost. Map each old URL to the closest matching new page with a 301 redirect, and only send pages to a parent when no closer match exists.

Is the Change of Address tool enough on its own?

No. The tool tells Google about the move, but it only works well when 301 redirects are already in place page to page. You also need both domains verified, a new sitemap, updated internal links and canonical tags, and a crawlable new site with no blocking rules left over from staging.

Is a change domain name SEO plan different for a small site?

The steps are the same, but the work is smaller. A site with a few dozen pages can map redirects by hand in an afternoon. Still verify both domains, use the Change of Address tool, submit a sitemap and test every redirect, because a small site has fewer pages to lose.
